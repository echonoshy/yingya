# Test the visual direction before expanding the film

Use this during authorized production for each new visual treatment and when
the user rejects the overall look. A complete manuscript or short runtime does
not establish visual quality. An existing verified treatment or focused revision
can reuse evidence while its relevant subjects, assets and motion remain valid.

## Choose the smallest meaningful sample

Select a scene, or a short scene-to-scene passage, that tests the central visual
idea. Include the main subject, actual visual assets, important motion and a
readable hold. Include an adjacent shot or transition when it carries the visual
idea. A decorative cover or static style image alone does not establish that the
story or motion works. A short film can use its first complete composition as
the passage; do not build a redundant preview project.

Use the intended output aspect ratio and local fonts. Resolve only the assets
and audio needed for this passage first. When narration or music drives the
design, inspect it with real audible material and measured timing; a silent
test cannot validate sound-led pacing. Label any temporary timing accurately.
Do not order every scene's media before validating a shared uncertain look.

## Build and inspect, then decide

Work within the approved composition and stable scene IDs. Keep one authoritative
schedule in `scenes.json`; testing a portion must not silently shorten the
requested deliverable or change the user-approved sequence. Use the existing
HyperFrames preview/check tools to inspect the passage over time and at relevant
frames. When a short render is necessary, use the production runner with its
actual supported arguments and a separate internal output path; do not assume
trim flags exist or publish the experiment as the complete review draft.

Open the actual visual evidence and inspect motion across the passage. Follow
[visual-review.md](visual-review.md) for composition, asset consistency, motion,
editing and sound. Check whether the passage demonstrates the promised visual
idea with real subjects and transformations. A generic title/card animation does
not validate a plan that promises illustration, environments or object motion.

Fix the highest-impact observed defect and re-inspect the affected part. Do not
invent an aesthetic score or run open-ended polish loops. After two targeted
repairs without resolving the same defect, preserve evidence and explain the
specific blocker; do not restart generation blindly. A change to the approved
concept or required media route must follow the existing scope-change process.

Record a concise outcome and evidence paths under the representative-scene
section of `.yingya/plan.md`: tested scene IDs/time range, actual evidence paths,
source identity, what was inspected, corrections and remaining limitations.
If reusing a prior treatment, record the evidence and why it still applies.
Update shared decisions in `DESIGN.md`
only when supported by the approved direction and the inspected result.
This is an internal production step, with no new manifest phase or checkpoint.
Show it separately only when the user requests a visual-direction review.

## Expand with continuity and variation

Reuse the validated subject treatment, type, color and motion vocabulary. Let
each scene's narrative role determine framing, layout, progression and duration.
Uniform branding does not require identical cards, entrances or scene lengths.

Inspect the assembled video as a sequence as well as individual frames. Remove
unnecessary repetition, check that each scene advances the message, and retain
the reading holds and intended emotional pacing. Then perform the full check,
render and immutable draft registration in [production.md](production.md).
A successful sample does not replace verification of the complete film.
